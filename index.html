What's happening

    Presets

    Situations (set the scene, light and camera settings)
    Cameras (only change camera settings: sensor, frame rate, shutter, aperture, ISO, ND)

    Exposure

    Aperture
    Size of the hole the light passes through. Lower f-number = bigger hole = more light and blurrier background.
    Shutter
    Shutter type
    How long the sensor collects light per frame. Angle is the fraction of a spinning disc that's open; 180° at 24 fps = 1/48 s, the classic film look. Blackmagic cameras let you pick either.
    ISO
    Doesn't add light or noise by itself. It shifts where middle grey sits in the sensor's range: higher ISO = brighter picture and less highlight headroom. Noise comes from too little light on the sensor, which people often raise ISO to compensate for. Dual-native sensors have a second base ISO with lower read noise.
    ND filter
    Sunglasses for the sensor. Cuts light without touching aperture or shutter.

    Lens & scene

    Scene
    What you're shooting. It's independent of scene brightness, so you can shoot a night scene at EV 15 and see it blown out, or a sunny scene at EV 3 and see it crushed.
    Focal length
    Wider = more in frame; longer = tighter and shallower depth of field.
    Focus distance
    Smaller sensor = tighter view at the same focal length and deeper focus.
    Scene brightness
    Measured in EV (at ISO 100). Every +1 EV is twice the light. It sets how much light there is, not what the scene looks like.
    Simplified teaching model: thin-lens optics, circle-of-confusion blur, and a stylised exposure/noise curve. Real cinema lenses are marked in T-stops (f-stops corrected for light lost in the glass), which run roughly 1/3 to 1/2 stop slower than the f-number. Sensor size here also affects field of view and depth of field.
